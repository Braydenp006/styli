I think Nexus and Itinerarium could complement each other really well without feeling like two versions of the same feature.
- Nexus shows you the relationships between your notes.
- Itinerarium lets you turn those relationships into a deliberate journey through your ideas.
The key distinction is that Nexus is discovery, while Itinerarium is direction.
The basic concept
Imagine you're collecting notes about consciousness, philosophy, and the universe. Your Nexus graph might reveal that several notes connect to one another.

But sometimes you don't want to explore randomly. You want to follow a particular line of thought, perhaps one you've been developing over several weeks.

That's where Itinerarium comes in.
1. Itinerarium as a path through your notes






















10











Rather than another canvas full of nodes, Itinerarium could be a linear, scrollable trail of notes.

Each Itinerarium has:
- A title
- A short description of what you're exploring
- An ordered sequence of notes
- Optional commentary between notes
- A way to branch into another path
For example:
ITINERARIUM / PHILOSOPHY
The nature of consciousness
A path through my thoughts on awareness and reality.

7 entries


1. What is consciousness?
   
   The original question that started this path.
2. The hard problem
   
   Why subjective experience is difficult to explain.
3. Emergence and complexity
   
   Could consciousness emerge from simpler systems?
4. The universe observing itself
   
   A speculative connection to cosmology.
5. Where this leaves me
   
   Your own synthesis or conclusion.

Each entry opens the original note. The path doesn't duplicate or move your notes.

This would be useful for research, essay preparation, personal reflection, or simply following a fascinating train of thought.
2. The important part: paths aren't folders
I wouldn't make Itinerarium another organizational hierarchy.

A note should be able to belong to:
- Your Tabularium, as its permanent home
- Several Nexus connections
- Multiple Itineraria
For instance, your note about emergence could appear in a path about consciousness, another about artificial intelligence, and another about the philosophy of science.

The Itinerarium is not where the note lives. It's how you choose to encounter it.

That distinction makes the feature genuinely valuable.
3. How users could create one
I'd support three ways of making an Itinerarium.

Curate a path

Start with a blank Itinerarium, then add notes in whatever order makes sense. Drag entries around, add commentary, and create branches.




Create from Nexus

Select a group of connected notes in the graph and turn them into an Itinerarium. You can then arrange them into a meaningful sequence.




Suggested paths

Styli could suggest a route through related notes based on links, tags, and recent writing. You decide whether to save it as an Itinerarium.



4. Make it feel like Styli, not a generic playlist
I would give Itinerarium a few distinctive features:
Waypoints
Each note in the path is a waypoint. You can mark certain notes as important stops, or add a brief explanation of why they belong in the sequence.
Branches
At certain points, a path could split into two directions.

For example, a path about consciousness might branch into neuroscience and philosophy. Both branches could eventually reconnect.

This is where Itinerarium could borrow a little from Nexus without becoming a graph itself.
Resumable journeys
You could mark where you last stopped and return later. An Itinerarium might show your progress through a research project or a collection of ideas you're still developing.
A final synthesis
At the end of a path, Styli could offer a special concluding entry: a note where you summarize what you've learned, what changed your mind, or what questions remain.

That would make Itinerarium more than a reading list. It becomes a record of how your understanding evolved.
